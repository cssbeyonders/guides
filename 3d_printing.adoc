== Using Fusion 360

Fusion 360 is a powerful application commonly used for 3D designing.

=== Sketch
[NOTE]
====
In Fusion, you need to understand the concept of creating a 3D object from 2D drawings called *Sketches*.

For example, if you were to draw a starfruit in Fusion, you would first draw a sketch of a star. This is because it is the cross section of it. Later, you can turn it into a 3D shape using functions that we will discuss later.

.Starfruit.
image::starfruit.jpg[Starfruit, 600, 400]
====

NOTE: Fusion contains lots of features. This guide does not cover all of them. In fact, by hovering over features in Fusion, it shows a short description about it. You can learn more about these functions by reading the descriptions and trying them out.

. To create a sketch, click *Create Sketch* from *Solid* > *Create*.
. Next, select a *Plane*. It can be one of the 3 preset planes at the origin, or flat surfaces of other 3D bodies.

+
NOTE: A plane is a 2D flat surface.

. The perspective will now switch to the orientation of the plane. Now you can start sketching.

==== Line

A line can be created by selecting the 2 endpoints of the line.

. Click *Line* under *Create*.
. Select 2 points to create a line.
. You can continue to select points to create multiple segments of lines.
. Alternatively, click another point and drag without releasing the click to create an arc.

[TIP]
====
Other methods to draw a line include:

- Midpoint Line

Other methods to draw a curve include:

- 3-point Arc
- Center Point Arc
- Tangent Arc
- Fit Point Spline
- Control Point Spline
- Conic Curve
====

==== Rectangle

A rectangle can be created by selecting 2 points. The 2 points are the opposite corners of the rectangle.

. Click *2-point Rectangle* under *Create*.
. Select 2 points to create the rectangle.

[TIP]
====
Other methods to draw a rectangle include:

- 3-point Rectangle
- Center Rectangle
====

==== Circle

A circle can be created by selecting its center and specifying its diameter.

. Click *Center Diameter Circle* under *Create*.
. Select its center.
. Enter its diameter, or alternatively select another point.

[TIP]
====
Other methods to draw a circle include:

- 2-point Circle
- 3-point Circle
- 2-tangent Circle
- 3-tangent Circle
====

==== Parameters

In large designs, if you want to change the size of certain things (e.g. hole size, board thickness), it is often difficult as many other things depend on previous items. However, Fusion offers a clean and efficient method for changing such values. Instead of entering the exact values when you create objects or perform actions, you can insert *Parameters* instead. Parameters in Fusion are like variables.

. Click *Change Parameters* under *Modify*.
. Click *+* on the top right corner.
. Enter a name and its expression for the parameter.

+
NOTE: An expression can be simply a value (e.g. 15) or depending on other parameters (e.g. length + 5).

. Click *OK*.

Now, when you write values of lengths, angles, etc., you can enter the name of the parameter to represent the value.

==== Sketch Dimensions and Constraints

Shapes are not placed in a random ray. *Sketch Dimensions* and *Constraints* fixes them.

To create a sketch dimension:
. Click *Sketch Dimension* under *Create*.
. Click a line to fix its length, a circle to fix its diameter, or two objets for their distances between.

Under the *Constriants* section, there are many types of constraints:

.Introduction of Various Constraints
[cols='1,3']
|===
|Name |Action

|Horizontal/Vertical
|Forces a line to be completely horizontal or vertical.

|Coincident
|Put two points together, or put a point on a line.

|Tangent
|Put a line on a curve or a circle such that the line just touches the side of the curve or circle.

|Equal
|Make two lengths always equal. When one changes, the other changes too.

|Parallel
|Make two lines parallel to each other (run in the same direction).

|Perpendicular
|Make two lines perpendicular to each other (form a 90 degree angle with each other).

|Fix/Unfix
|When something is fixed, it position and size can never change.

|Midpoint
|Put a point on the midpoint of another line, or make two lines intersect at their midpoints.

|Concentric
|Make two circles, arcs or ellipses share the same center. Similar to putting their centers together.

|Collinear
|Make two line segments share a common line (that goes infinitely).

|Symmetry
|Select two objects that you want to have symmetry with, then select a mirroring line. The two objects will now mirror each
other across the line.

|Curvature
|Select a spline and another line, spline or arc such that their curves continue smoothly.

|Polygon
|Ignore this.

|===

You can change the nozzle size and printer in the top left corner.

NOTE: The nozzle size is the diameter of the extruded filament. A smaller number results in a finer object printed.

TIP: It is found out that a smaller nozzle size can also reduce the weight due to reduced wall thickness. Therefore, 0.4 nozzle is better for most situations.

+

[#changing-filament]
=== Changing Filament

. Go to *Control* > *Extrude/Retract* > *Retract*.

. Unlock the lock.

. Hold down the white part and pull the tube out.

. From the rack, pull the filament. While pulling, check if the filament on the nozzle side also gets pulled along.

+
If not, that means there are broken segments of filament within the tube. Push the filament instead of pulling. The segments should drop out from the nozzle side. After every segment drop, pull again to check if there are any remaining segments.

. Pull the filament all the way out and take away the filament roll.

. Place a new filament roll onto the rack and insert it into the tube.

+
IMPORTANT: Make sure that the filament and the tube goes through the hole on the rack.

. After it has reached the nozzle side, use a pair of pliers to cut the end such that it is tipped

+
TIP: This can make it easier to reach the bottom of the nozzle.

. Hold down the white part. Insert *only the filament* into the nozzle and to the very bottom.

. Go to *Control* > *Extrude/Retract* > *Extrude*. While extruding, push down the filament at the nozzle.

. If the extrusion is successful, hold down the white part and insert the tube.

. *Lock the lock.*

+
IMPORTANT: Locking the lock is very important. Otherwise, the nozzle will not be intaking.

+

[#could-not-print-out-filament]
==== Could not Print Out Filament

The primary reason is that the supply of filament was interrupted but the printer could not detect the running out/blocking of filament supply.

There are many reasons this could happen. 

.Causes for Interruption of Filament Supply
[cols="a,a,a"]
|===
|Possible Causes |Solutions |Remarks

|Lock is unlocked.
|Lock the lock.
|

|Filament not fully inserted.
|Push down the filament to the *very* bottom.
|There are multiple "layers" within the nozzle. What you thought was the bottom may not be the real bottom.

|Filament ran out.
|<<changing-filament, Change filament.>>
|

|Broken pieces of filament in tube.
|<<changing-filament, Remove broken pieces.>>
|
[disc]
- If the filament is easy to break, and there are tight turns in the tube, it is easy for this to happen.
- Filaments that are not stored in proper environment are easier to break.

|There are filaments of other types stuck in the nozzle.
|
. On the screen, go to *Control* > *Extrude/Retract*.
. Configure it to a high temperature (like 260ºC).
. Press *Extrude*.
. While it is extruding, push down new filament into the nozzle.
. Take note of the extruded filament. If there are filament of other colors, repeat extruding until it is fully gone.
|This is because filaments like ABS require a higher temperature to melt. When we extrude using a higher temperature, it can melt the stuck filament.

|The filament tied in the filament roll.
|Untie/cut the filament.
|

|The temperature is not suitable for the filament in use.
|Go back to the software and <<multicolor-and-filament-selection, configure the filament to be correct>>.
|

|===

== Glossary
Extrusion::
. [In Fusion] The action of turning a 2D sketch into a 3D object by linear action.
. [In 3D Printer, Action] The action of ejecting filament from the nozzle.
. [In 3D Printer, Physical Object] The metal part at the back of the magnetic board for fixing the location of the plate.
Filament:: The material for 3D printing. It comes in rolls.
Grabber:: One of the two spots on the plate for you to grab and remove the plate.
Magnetic board:: An object in a 3D printer where the plate is fixed on.
Notch:: The indented part at the back of the plate where the extrusion is fitted.
Nozzle:: The apparatus in a 3D printer which intakes filament, heats it up, and extrudes it.
Plate:: The board in a 3D printer where 3D printing is done on.
Rack:: The place where filament rolls are stored.
Screen:: The display on the 3D printer.
Slice:: The action of turning a 3D model into layers for the 3D printer to print.
Support:: Extra printed filament for avoid hanging parts being printed in the air.
Tube:: The white plastic flexible cylinder connecting the rack and the nozzle of the printer to guide filament
